Exercise 5.

Consider the familiar graph of \(f(x) = \frac{1}{x}\text{,}\) which has a vertical asypmtote at \(x = 0\) and a horizontal asymptote at \(y = 0\text{,}\) as pictured in Figure 5.1.8. In addition, consider the similarly-shaped function \(g\) shown in Figure 5.1.9, which has vertical asymptote \(x = -1\) and horizontal asymptote \(y = -2\text{.}\)

  1. How can we view \(g\) as a transformation of \(f\text{?}\) Explain, and state how \(g\) can be expressed algebraically in terms of \(f\text{.}\)

  2. Find a formula for \(g\) as a function of \(x\text{.}\) What is the domain of \(g\text{?}\)

  3. Explain algebraically (using the form of \(g\) from (b)) why \(\lim_{x \to \infty} g(x) = -2\) and \(\lim_{x \to -1^+} g(x) = \infty\text{.}\)

  4. What if a function \(h\) (again of a similar shape as \(f\)) has vertical asymptote \(x = 5\) and horizontal asymtote \(y = 10\text{.}\) What is a possible formula for \(h(x)\text{?}\)

  5. Suppose that \(r(x) = \frac{1}{x+35} - 27\text{.}\) Without using a graphing utility, how do you expect the graph of \(r\) to appear? Does it have a horizontal asymptote? A vertical asymptote? What is its domain?
