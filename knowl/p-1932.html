From the data in Table 3.1.10, we see that the average rate of change is increasing as we increase the value of \(t\text{.}\) We naturally say that \(f\) appears to be “increasing at an increasing rate”. For the function \(g\text{,}\) we first notice that its average rate of change is always negative, but also that the average rate of change gets less negative as we increase the value of \(t\text{.}\) Said differently, the average rate of change of \(g\) is also increasing as we increase the value of \(t\text{.}\) Since \(g\) is always decreasing but its average rate of change is increasing, we say that \(g\) appears to be “decreasing at an increasing rate”.
