It's also often possible to view given information in the context of the unit circle. With the earlier given information that \(\csc(\alpha) = \frac{5}{3}\text{,}\) it's natural to view \(\alpha\) as being the angle in a right triangle that lies opposite a leg of length \(3\) with the hypotenuse being \(5\text{,}\) since \(\csc(\alpha) = \frac{\text{hyp}}{\text{opp}}\text{.}\) The Pythagorean Theorem then tells us the leg adjacent to \(\alpha\) has length \(4\text{,}\) as seen in \(\triangle OPQ\) in Figure 4.5.3.
