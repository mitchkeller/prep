Our observations generalize for any positive constant \(k \gt 0\text{.}\) In the case where \(k = 2\text{,}\) we saw that the period of \(g(t) = \sin(2t)\) is \(P = \frac{1}{2} \cdot 2\pi\text{,}\) whereas in the case where \(k = \frac{1}{2}\text{,}\) the period of \(h(t) = \sin(\frac{1}{2}t)\) is \(P = 2 \cdot 2\pi = \frac{1}{\frac{1}{2}} \cdot 2\pi\text{.}\) Identical reasoning holds if we are instead working with the cosine function.
