From the viewpoint of Figure 4.2.3, as the point corresponding to angle \(t\) traverses the circle and generates the point \((a,b)\text{,}\) we know \(\cos(t) = a\) and \(\sin(t) = b\text{,}\) and therefore the tangent function tracks the ratio of these two quantities, and is given by

\begin{equation*} \tan(t) = \frac{\sin(t)}{\cos(t)} = \frac{b}{a}\text{.} \end{equation*}

From the perspective of any right triangle (not necessarily in the unit circle) with hypotenuse “hyp” and legs “adj” and “opp” that are respectively adjacent and opposite the known angle \(\theta\text{,}\) as seen in Figure 4.2.4, we know that \(\sin(\theta) = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}\) and \(\cos(\theta) = \frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}\text{.}\) Substituting these expressions for \(\sin(\theta)\) and \(\cos(\theta)\) in the rule for the tangent function, we see that

\begin{equation*} \tan(\theta) = \frac{\sin(\theta)}{\cos(\theta)} = \frac{\frac{\text{opp}}{\text{hyp}}}{\frac{\text{adj}}{\text{hyp}}} = \frac{\text{opp}}{\text{adj}}\text{.} \end{equation*}
