By the algebraic rule for \(m\text{,}\) \(m(x) = 2r(x+1)-1\text{.}\) In words, this means that given an input \(x\) for \(m\text{,}\) we do the following processes in this particular order:

  1. add \(1\) to \(x\) and then apply the function \(r\) to the quantity \(x+1\text{;}\)

  2. multiply the output of \(r(x+1)\) by \(2\text{;}\)

  3. subtract \(1\) from the output of \(2r(x+1)\text{.}\)

These three steps correspond to three basic transformations: (1) shift the graph of \(r\) to the left by \(1\) unit; (2) stretch the resulting graph vertically by a factor of \(2\text{;}\) (3) shift the resulting graph vertically by \(-1\) units. We can see the graphical impact of these algebraic steps by taking them one at a time. In Figure 1.8.14, we see the function \(p\) that results from a shift \(1\) unit left of the parent function in Figure 1.8.13. (Each time we take an additional step, we will de-emphasize the preceding function by having it appear in lighter color and dashed.)
