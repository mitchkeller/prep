SOLUTION Both \(P\) and \(Q\) lie on \(y = x^2 +2\) , so their coordinates must satisfy that equation. Point \(Q\) has \(x\)-coordinate 2, so \(y = 2^2 + 2 = 6\text{.}\) \(P\) has \(y\)-coordinate 13, so
\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} 13 \amp = x^2 + 2, \\ x^2 \amp = 11, \quad\mbox{so} \\ x \amp = \pm \sqrt{11}. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
Thus \(x = - \sqrt{11}\) because we know from the graph that \(x \lt 0\text{.}\) Using coordinates of \(P\) and \(Q\text{,}\) we know that
\begin{equation*} m = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{13-6}{- \sqrt{11} - 2} = \frac{7}{- \sqrt{11} - 2}. \end{equation*}
Since \(y = 6\) when \(x = 2\text{,}\) we have
\begin{equation*} y = \left( \frac{7}{- \sqrt{11} - 2} \right) (x-2) + 6. \end{equation*}
