If we rearrange the statements of the facts in Equation (3.4.1), we can see yet another important relationship between the powers of \(10\) and base-\(10\) logarithm function. Noting that \(\log_{10}(100) = 2\) and \(100 = 10^2\) are equivalent statements, and substituting the latter equation into the former, we see that

\begin{equation} \log_{10}(10^2) = 2\text{.}\tag{3.4.3} \end{equation}

In words, Equation (3.4.3) says that “the power to which we raise \(10\) to get \(10^2\text{,}\) is \(2\)”. That is, the base-\(10\) logarithm function undoes the work of the powers of \(10\) function.
